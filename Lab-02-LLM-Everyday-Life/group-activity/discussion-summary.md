# Discussion Summary: Group 7



## How we used the LLM
We used an LLM (Claude) as a product-development partner. The steps were:
1. Understanding the problem and clarifying who the user is.
2. Brainstorming product ideas.
3. Choosing one idea (the smart backpack) and developing it into a full concept.
4. Asking the LLM to critique the concept.
5. Producing mock-ups of the bag and the companion app.
6. Preparing the presentation outline.

The prompts and answers are in `../screenshots/` (1 to 7).

## Ideas considered
| Idea | Why we did / did not choose it |
|---|---|
| **Smart backpack with app (chosen)** | Solves carrying, organising and forgetting in one product; physical and digital parts support each other |
| Digital-only packing checklist app | Cheap, but it cannot weigh or track items, and does not make carrying more comfortable |
| Modular detachable pouch system | Useful, kept as one feature of the backpack |
| Campus smart lockers | Reduces carrying, but depends on the university building and installing lockers |
| Foldable laptop-and-tablet device combining notebooks | Reduces items, but too costly for most students |
| Shared "leave-at-campus" storage service | Good for heavy books, but needs staff and space |

## Weaknesses found (from the critical-review step)
- Extra electronics make the bag heavier and more expensive.
- Bluetooth tags need charging or battery replacement.
- Students may not keep their timetable up to date.
- Battery safety rules for bags with built-in power banks.
- Privacy: location alerts must not track the student unnecessarily.

## What we learned about using LLMs for design
**Strengths**
- Fast brainstorming of many different ideas.
- Good at structuring a concept (name, pitch, features, target user).
- Useful as a critic when asked to look for weak points.

**Limitations**
- It gives confident but generic suggestions. The group had to decide what was realistic and affordable.
- It does not know real prices, student habits or campus rules, so these need real research.
- The visual mock-ups are concept illustrations, not professional designs. A designer would still be needed.
- Answers should be checked, for example any numbers about safe bag weight.
